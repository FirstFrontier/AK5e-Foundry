# Chapter 1: Changing the Game
In order to make this overhaul easy to parse for players of Fifth Edition D&D, as few already existent mechanics as possible were changed. Some changes, however, were necessary, to better represent the feeling of Arknights.
:
Furthermore, there are naturally plenty of new mechanics for the same reason. As such, this Chapter is divided into two sections; mechanics that have been changed, and mechanics that are new.

{{note
##### A Note on Balance
Due to the addition of things such as Operator Skills, and the ability to customise your "build" in a way 5e typically does not allow, Player Characters in this overhaul are expected to be a fair sight stronger than typical 5e Player Characters, especially if "min-maxing" is involved.
:
If you are a DM, please keep this in mind.
}}

:
## Changes to Old Mechanics
This section includes many of the traditional 5e mechanics that have been changed for the purpose of this overhaul. Weapons and Equipment have also been changed, but they are covered under their own section.

### Conditions
Two existing Conditions have been changed for game balance;
:
#### Frightened
A Creature that is Frightened can either Move, take an Action, or take a Bonus Action during their Turn. Some effects may also specify other Actions a Creature has to take while Frightened, such as moving away from the source.
:
#### Surprised
A Creature that is Surprised can either Move, take an Action, or take a Bonus Action during their Turn.

### Death Saving Throws

The following changes have been made to the Death Saving Throw mechanic. 

##### Dying State

When a Creature that can make Death Saving Throws is reduced to 0 Hit Points, they do not fall Unconscious, and instead enter the **Dying** state. Additionally, a Creature no longer accumulates successful Death Saving Throws, and does not automatically stabilise after three successful Saves or recieving any healing. While a Creature is in the Dying State, the actions it can take are heavily restricted, and is only able to take Death's Door Actions, detailed below, by expending Mettle during their Turn. The Creature makes a Death Saving Throw at the beginning of each of its Turns, as well as when it takes Damage while at 0 Hit Points. 


##### Mettle
Mettle is the "currency" required to act in the **Dying** state, spent on Death's Door Actions. It can only be gained by a Creature who is **Dying**, and is lost when that Creature is no longer **Dying.** The maximum Mettle a Creature can have is six. 


\column


##### Gaining Mettle

When a **Dying** Creature makes its Death Saving Throw at the beginning of its Turn, it gains 2 Mettle for a successful Save, and 1 Mettle for a failed Save. 
:
Additionally, at the end of each Turn, a Creature gains Mettle based on the following conditions:
- +1 Mettle if they regained Hit Points
- +1 Mettle for every 10 Hit Points they regained in a single instance.
- +6 Mettle if a Creature within 5 feet of them succeeds on a DC15 Medicine Check on them as an Action.

##### Death's Door Actions
A **Dying** Creature does not act normally during its Turn. Instead, it takes Death's Door Actions by expending Mettle; these Actions all do not require any Action, and so can performed as many times in a Creature's Turn as wanted, so long as the Creature can pay the cost in Mettle. The Death's Door Actions are as follows:

- **Lash Out (1 Mettle).** You make a single Attack.

- **Stagger Away (1 Mettle).** You move up to half your Speed. 

- **Take Action (2 Mettle).** You take an Action or Bonus Action that would normally be available to you. 

- **Stabilise (6 Mettle).** You settle the injury enough to live another day. At the beginning of your next Turn, you do not need to make a Death Saving Throw, you leave the **Dying** state, and if you have 0 Hit Points, you regain 1 Hit Point. 

##### Failed Death Saves and Death
A Creature dies when it has incurred three failed Death Saving Throws, or when it takes a single instance of Damage in excess of that Creature's current Hit Points + maximum Hit Points. Some effects may also directly kill Creatures, bypassing Death Saving Throws entirely.
:
Removing Death Saving Throws requires rest. Each time a Creature completes a Long Rest, it may remove one of its failed Death Saving Throws.

{{note
##### Author Note
I don't often feel the need to explain why some decisions have been made for this conversion. However, I know this is likely to draw questions as to why I did it. 
:
I do also want to add that if you would rather play with normal Fifth Edition Death Saving Throw rules, you absolutely can, but please bear in mind that some mechanics may not function correctly.
:
###### For Balance
Healing and Hit Points in Fifth Edition is a strange balancing act. Common gaming logic dictates that healing should be used to keep characters at high health, as a preventative medicine; which is often the wrong decision. While it can work, especially at higher levels where enemies hit extremely hard, hit points of non-tanky characters evaporate quickly, leaving them downed. However, they can be immediately restored to complete functionality, with essentially no further consequences, by any amount of healing, such as a Level 1 spell like Healing Word. Any attempt to give them more HP than that is essentially a waste; it still won't allow them to survive another round of attacks without going down, and as long as they are conscious during their turn, little else matters.
:
I dub this "reverse whack-a-mole", and I find it very immersion breaking; a character hitting 0 Hit Points should be a major deal from a narrative perspective. However, the ready availability of ranged healing and the limited consequences for becoming downed mean it's really not a big deal. Additionally, it also renders physical healing, in the form of the Medicine Skill, essentially useless. In the world of Arknights, with Medicine being a core theme, that is untenable. 
:
###### For Theme 
That nicely segues me into the second point; healing magic, or Healing Arts as they are called for our purposes here are no substitute for traditional medicine and trained professionals. Doctors, medicine, and healthcare are key themes for Arknights; its deuteragonist is a healthcare practioner herself, Dr. Kal'tsit. Medicine needs to be important for this conversion. 
:
Also, people in Arknights do not often fight to the death unless cornered. When an Operator reaches 0 HP in game, they do not die; they retreat, are presumably healed behind friendly lines, and return to the fight a time later. Even some enemies and bosses don't die when their HP falls to 0. They retreat to lick their wounds and try again another time.
:
Equally, however, people **do** die in combat or otherwise in Arknights. One of the very first things that happens in the game is the death of several Operators in combat. Several notable enemies have died fighting Rhodes Island for a cause they particularly believed in, or a path they were harried down. 
:
With all that in mind, I created these rules, to represent these aspects; medicine is important, healing magic is not a one-size fits all solution, and if characters find themselves getting low, they should retreat before they meet their end. 
}}

### No ASI Replacement Feats
When you would gain an Ability Score Improvement, you cannot exchange that improvement for a Feat.

### No Healing for Constructs
Creatures of the Construct Creature Type cannot restore Hit Points as a result of Healing Arts, Operator Skills, Class Features, or use of the Medicine Skill unless specified otherwise.

### No Multiclassing
Multiclassing within AK5e is not permitted by default. If your GM wishes to allow this, be aware that no design decisions have been made with this in mind.
:
In its place is the ability to have Alternate Classes, described under the New Mechanics section.

### Physical/Arts Damage
As in 5th Edition, Damage dealt can be divided into physical and magical damage.
The following table indicates which Damage Types are considered to be Physical or Arts (unless stated otherwise):

| Physical Damage | Arts Damage |
|:---------:|:-----------:|
| Bludgeoning | Acid
| Slashing    | Cold
| Piercing    | Fire
| Thunder     | Force
|             | Lightning
|             | Necrotic
|             | Poison
|             | Psychic
|             | Radiant

{{align-text:left
All Damage dealt by Arts is considered to be Arts Damage.
}}



### Skills and Proficiencies
Some traditional 5e Skills are not likely to be useful in the world of Terra, or have such niche application as to be not worth considering. 

- **Intelligence (Religion)** and **Intelligence (History)** have been combined into **Intelligence (Lore)**.
- **Wisdom (Survival)** and **Intelligence (Nature)** have been combined into **Wisdom (Wilderness)**.
- **Intelligence (Arcana)** has been renamed to **Intelligence (Arts)**.
- **Intelligence (Technology)** (Shortened to **Intelligence (Tech)**) has been introduced. This Skill governs interaction with all forms of machinery and technology, from mechanical to electrical.

{{align-text:left
If you would gain Proficiency or Expertise in a Skill that has been combined into another, you instead gain it in the respective combined Skill.
}}





### Spellcasting
How Creatures cast Spells (henceforth referred to as "Arts" in keeping with Arknights nomenclature) has not changed. However, the resource they spend in order to do so has.
:
Creatures no longer have Spell Slots, but instead have Arts Points, the exact amount of which is determined mostly by Class, or sometimes Branch, Level (in the case of Player Characters) or GM Discretion (in the case of Non Player Characters).
:
Casting an Art requires the caster to pay a cost in Arts Points equal to the Level they wish to cast the Art at. If they do not have enough Arts Points, they cannot cast the Art at that Level.
:
The maximum Level at which a caster can use an Art is controlled by their Character Level or CR; while a Creature will have enough Arts Points to cast a 9th Level Art fairly early into their career, they cannot do so until their Maximum Art Level is 9.
{{note
##### 9th Level Arts
9th Level Arts are the peak of what a Caster can achieve, capable of world changing events. For this reason, a Creature can only cast a single 9th Level Art per Long Rest, regardless of remaining Arts Points.
}}
The rate at which Creatures recover expended Arts Points depends on their Class, but generally speaking it will either be upon completing a Short Rest, or a Long Rest.

### Spellcasting: Cantrips
The first time each Turn a Creature rolls Damage for a damaging Cantrip (a Cantrip that directly deals Damage to one or more Creature(s), such as Firebolt or Thunderclap), they add their Spellcasting Ability Modifier to that Damage Roll.

## New Mechanics
This section includes all the major new mechanics introduced for AK5e.
:
### Damage Reduction (DR)
Damage Reduction (DR) is a flat reduction to the Damage a Creature takes. It is applied **before** Resistances/Vulnerability, and can be specific to a particular Damage Type(s) (such as Fire), a class of Damage Types (such as Physical), or all Damage.
It cannot reduce the damage a Creature takes to less than 1.

### Martial DC
Many Class and Branch Features make reference to a Character’s “Martial DC”. A Character’s Martial DC is equal to 8 + their Strength or Dexterity Bonus (their Choice) + their Proficiency Bonus.

### New Conditions
There are currently eight new Conditions introduced for AK5e, as follows:
:
##### Bleeding
:
A Bleeding Creature loses 2d6 Hit Points at the beginning of their Turn. This Condition is removed if the Creature recovers any missing Hit Points. It can also be removed by a Creature within 5ft succeeding on a DC12 Medicine Check as an Action. Only living Creatures can Bleed.
:
##### Burning
:
A Burning Creature takes 2d8 Fire Damage at the beginning of their Turn. A Creature can remove this Condition as an Action while they have the Prone Condition. Other ways of removing the Condition (such as submerging in water) are allowed at DM discretion.
:
##### Corroded
:
Any DR a Corroded Creature has is halved (rounding down). In addition, their AC is reduced by 2.
:
##### Dazed
:
A Dazed Creature subtracts 1d4 from its Attack Rolls and Saving Throws.
:
##### Frozen
:
A Frozen Creature has its Movement Speed reduced by 20ft. Additionally, it has Disadvantage on any Dexterity Saving Throws.
:
##### Necrotised
:
A Necrotised Creature suffers a -3 to all Damage Rolls it makes. Additionally, when they cause another Creature to make a Saving Throw, the DC of that Saving Throw is reduced by 3. 
:
##### Nervous Impairment
:
A Creature with Nervous Impairment can either Move or take an Action on each of its Turns. Additionally, whenever it is hit by an Attack, it loses 3 Hit Points. 
:
##### Silenced
:
A Creature that is Silenced cannot cast any Arts, or use any Ability that is noted as being an Art.

### Operator Skills

At 2nd Level, 7th Level, and 14th Level, you gain a Basic, Advanced, and Elite Operator Skill respectively from the Skill Tables available to your Class (found after the Class Features for each Class). Some Branches may provide additional options for Skill selection; some may require you to select an Operator Skill from a different table. 
:
Operator Skills have the following properties:

- **Name:** The name of the Skill.
- **SP Cost:** How many Skill Points (SP) are required to Activate that Skill. Your maximum amount of Skill Points is equal to the highest Required SP of all your current Skills. 
- **Start SP:** Whenever you finish a Long Rest, if you have less Skill Points than this Value, you gain Skill Points until you have this value. If you have multiple Skills with a Starting SP, use the highest one.
- **Charging Method:** Each Skill allows you to gain Skill Points in one of three ways. You cannot gain Skill Points if you currently have an Active Skill, from an Attack made or Art cast as part of a Skill, from Damage dealt by a Skill, or by Hit Points restored by a Skill.
  - **Automatic:** You gain 2 Skill Points at the Beginning of your Turn in combat.
  - **Offensive:** You gain 1 Skill Point whenever you do any of the following during your Turn in combat:
    - Deal Damage to a hostile Creature; this can only occur once per Attack. 
    - Restore Hit Points to a Creature except yourself.
    - Grant Temporary Hit Points to a Creature (except yourself) with an Art. 
  - **Defensive:** You gain 2 Skill Points whenever a hostile Creature makes an Attack against you, or you take Damage from a hostile Creature through a non-Attack source.

{{note,wide
##### Stacking Charging Methods
Each of your current Skills allows you to gain SP through its associated method, and these methods can stack. If you had 2 Offensive Charge Skills, you would gain 2 SP if you damaged a Creature with an Attack.
}}


- **Activation Cost:** The Action Cost required to Activate that Skill. Typically, this is either Action, Bonus Action, Reaction, or no Action required. 
- **Effect:** What the Skill does upon being Activated. Note that if a Skill calls for something you do not have access to without an alternative, such as a Spell Save DC, you cannot use that Operator Skill.
- **Duration:** The length of time that Skill remains Active. 

{{note,wide
##### One Skill at a Time
A Character can only have one Operator Skill active at a time, even if they have the Skill Points to activate another.
}}

### Optional Rule: Alternate Classes

Beginning at 7th Level, if the DM allows, you can choose an Alternate Class. This is to represent you choosing a different path with which to apply your extensive combat experience, as many Operators in Arknights do.
:
There are two steps to choosing an Alternate Class:

1) Pick a Class that is different to the one you already have.
2) Once you have picked the Class, you may select two of your Ability Scores (e.g Strength and Wisdom); while you are in your Alternate Class, the value of those two Ability Scores are swapped. 

{{align-text:left
As implied, your original Class and Alternate Class do not exist simultaneously. Instead, you can switch between the two as part of a Long Rest. However, if you do so, you do not regain any Hit Dice as part of that Long Rest.
:
While in your Alternate Class, all of your Ability Scores (except the ones you chose to swap) remain the same, and you retain your inventory, but your Maximum Hit Points, Movement Speed, and Features are completely separate. Your Alternate Class is always the same Level as your original Class.
}}
:
{{note
##### Author's Note
Essentially, an Alternate Class is a separate character sheet with the same name, character history, and inventory.
:
It is still the same person, and thus all of their deeds and reputation remain; it is purely their style of combat that has changed.
}}

### Oripathy

Oripathy is an endemic condition to the world of Terra. It is a progressive, terminal disease caused by direct or prolonged exposure to active Originium. Its sufferers are known simply as "Infected", and suffer great pain on the daily, both physically and socially, as Originium stones grow within their body and disrupt their physiology, while other folk actively discriminate against and persecute them.
:
Even in death, they are not free from the disease. Those Infected who perish will, at some point after their death, fully crystallise before disintegrating into Originium Dust; this dust is highly infectious, and is a major transmission vector for Oripathy. This process can take days, or it can take a few minutes; it can also be a gradual disintegration, or an explosive one, scattering the particulate matter far and wide. As such, while the Infected themselves are not a transmission vector, they are extremely dangerous to be around when they die for the uninfected.
:
The disease can be controlled, but with current technological advancements cannot be cured. Furthermore, controlling the disease either requires specialist knowledge or expensive suppressant drugs, many of which are not available to the countless sufferers worldwide.
:
One "advantage" of Oripathy is that it allows its sufferers to cast Arts without an Arts Unit, by using the Originium in their own bodies as a catalyst, often to extreme effect. However, doing so accelerates the progression of the disease, sometimes rapidly, leading to a vastly reduced life expectancy.

\column

#### Infected Creatures and Characters
If a Creature is Infected, it may cast Arts without using a Casting Unit. Casting Arts like this is considered to have the Conductive (3) Special Property. 
:
However, doing so accelerates the progression of their disease; there are no hard and fast consequences for this, but there should be narrative consequences for doing so, eventually resulting in death.
:
{{note
#### Optional Rules: Oripathy
I am of the personal belief that Oripathy works best as a narrative device without mechanical consequences, but I am also aware that many people struggle with such open-ended mechanics, as it very much depends on your personal imagination, as well as that of the DM. For that reason, here are two variants of Oripathy with actual mechanical consequences.

##### Optional Rule: Oripathy Fatigue
If a Creature is Infected, it may cast Arts without using a Casting Unit. Casting Arts like this is considered to have the Conductive (5) Special Property. 
:
However, each time they do so, their maximum Hit Points decrease by 1d10. These missing Hit Points cannot be recovered.

##### Optional Rule: Oripathy Hardcore
Infected Creatures have an Originium Assimilation Value, which begins at 0. If this number is ever higher than their Constitution Score, they die. 
:
Infected Creatures may cast Arts without using a Casting Unit. Casting Arts like this is considered to have the Conductive (5) Special Property. 
:
However, each time they do so, they roll 1d4, and add it to their Originium Assimilation Value. 
:
Additionally, every two weeks they go without successfully supressing their Oripathy in some way, they roll 1d4 and add it to their Originium Assimilation Value.

}}

### Rest Actions
Many Features in AK5e, especially Tool Kits, reference Rest Actions, either as a "Short Rest Action" or "Long Rest Action". These are actions that you carry out over the course of their respective Rest, and provide some benefit if you complete them. 
:
To complete a Rest Action, you must complete the Rest while performing that Rest Action for the duration of that Rest. You can only perform one Rest Action at time.
:
You can perform a Short Rest Action as if it were a Long Rest Action, but you cannot perform a Long Rest Action during a Short Rest.
